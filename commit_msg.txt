Phase 3: fix relaxation-walk EMRI overproduction, validate all four initial conditions

Continues Phase 3 from the previous commit's "EMRI rate far too high" blocker
(757/1000 EMRIs, 3 mergers vs. Table 1's 371 for H18). Two independently-justified
fixes plus one unrelated bug fix bring this down substantially, though a genuine,
documented open question remains in the heavier-mass initial conditions.

Fix 1 - Genuine substepping of the relaxation random walk
- _apply_relaxation_walk (simulation.py) no longer aggregates an entire timestep's
  relaxation kick as one Gaussian evaluated at the timestep's starting semimajor
  axis. It now breaks each timestep into IntegrationConfig.relaxation_substeps
  (default 20) sub-steps, re-deriving local dynamics (period, t_relax, v_circ) at
  the BH's current radius between them.
- Measured effect: 75.7% -> 64.5% EMRI at N=1000. A 1-2000 substep-count scan
  showed no further improvement past this -- substep coarseness was not the
  dominant cause of the original problem, but the fix is real and kept.

Fix 2 - Re-derived Eq. 22's <M_avg>/rho and the Coulomb logarithm from primary
sources, independent of EMRI-rate matching (per explicit instruction)
- <M_avg>/rho switched from a BH-inclusive weighted average to star-only
  (1 Msun, rho_star), per Rose et al. 2022 Eq. 10's explicit "single-mass
  system" statement -- the paper this exact mechanism is credited to.
- Coulomb logarithm recomputed as ln(M_bullet/M_star) ~= 15.2 (was a 10.0
  placeholder), per Bar-Or, Kupi & Alexander 2013 and Vasiliev 2017, both
  fetched and read directly -- the standard prescription specifically for
  relaxation dominated by a central point mass.
- Measured effect: 64.5% -> 34-37% EMRI, merger channel revived (400-580
  mergers per run, vs. near-zero before).
- This resolution is contested, not settled: N26's own Eq. 22 phrasing (which
  drops Rose et al. 2022's "single-mass system" qualifier) plus Eq. 23's
  explicit star-only override (redundant if Eq. 22 already defaulted to
  star-only) argue for the opposite (BH-inclusive) reading. Both readings were
  empirically tested; BH-inclusive reproduces the original defect almost
  exactly (68.8% EMRI, zero mergers). Star-only was kept because it's the only
  one that leaves the merger channel functioning. Full trace, including the
  page-image verification of both equations, in
  paper/limitations.md#average-object-mass.

Bug fix - mean_bh_mass placeholder
- PopulationConfig.mean_bh_mass had been left at a flat, unchecked 20.0 for all
  four initial conditions. Actual Monte Carlo sampler means: K20 9.72, K20+M
  9.94, H18 33.40, H18+M 34.20 -- off by up to 40% in both directions. Fixed;
  default updated to H18+M's actual mean (34.2).

New finding - systematic runaway growth in H18/H18+M (open, not resolved)
- With both fixes applied, every one of 6 independent H18/H18+M seeds produces
  at least one BH that grows to 3,556-89,870 Msun over 55-231 merger
  generations, vs. Table 1's 407.3 Msun max / 12-16G. Traced directly: both the
  stellar-collision (Eq. 18) and GW-capture (Eq. 4-7) channels have genuine,
  correctly-implemented superlinear mass-dependent positive feedback once a BH
  is massive and sits in the dense inner cluster -- verified against a direct
  high-resolution page-image render of Eq. 4-6, not a transcription bug.
  Quantified precisely across all 6 seeds: it's a robust, moderate heavy tail
  (44-58 BHs/1000 exceed 200 Msun consistently), not 1-3 rare outliers, with
  only the single most extreme BH per run responsible for the eye-catching
  10,000+ Msun figures. K20/K20+M show no such runaway in any of 6 seeds and
  validate well against Table 1 (mergers/max mass within ~1.3-1.5x).
- Logged as a genuinely open scientific question (not a bug left unfixed): it
  traces back to the same unresolved Eq. 22 tension above, which controls how
  effectively relaxation can evict a growing BH from the runaway-conducive
  region before either channel's feedback runs away with it. Full mechanism
  trace, per-seed numbers, and ruled-out alternative explanations (stellar
  depletion, ejection rate) in paper/limitations.md#phase2-emri-rate-high.
