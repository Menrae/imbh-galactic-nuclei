Phase 5 complete: SMBH-mass generalization scan -- both of N26's own Section 5.4
open questions now answered

This commit closes out Phase 5 (the last planned physics-validation phase) and is a
natural checkpoint for the project as a whole, so this message recaps all of it, not
just this diff.

WHAT THIS PROJECT IS

An independent semianalytic reimplementation of Newton et al. 2026 (ApJ 1006, 184,
"N26" throughout), which models intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) formation in
galactic nuclei via two channels acting on a population of stellar-mass black holes
orbiting a central SMBH: repeated stellar collisions (gravitational focusing driven)
and gravitational-wave capture mergers. The goal is not just to reproduce N26's
Table 1, but to independently re-derive every equation from its cited primary
sources, catch and document every place the paper's text is ambiguous or
underspecified, and use the resulting validated pipeline to answer both of N26's own
explicitly-stated open questions (Section 5.4). End target: a direct, evidence-based
correspondence with N26's authors (Fred Rasio's group, CIERA) once that write-up is
ready.

PHASES 0-2 -- scaffolding, physics, integration loop

Every equation from N26's Section 4 physics (velocity dispersion, BH/stellar density
profiles, collision and GW-capture timescales, spin evolution, EMRI stopping
conditions, relaxation-driven orbital random walk) implemented from primary sources
and independently unit-tested. The full adaptive-timestep simulation loop -- sample
an initial population, evolve collisions/mergers/EMRIs/ejections forward in time,
track merger trees -- runs end to end for N=1000, 10 Gyr.

PHASE 3 -- validation against N26's published Table 1

All four of N26's initial-condition recipes (K20, K20+M, H18, H18+M) implemented and
validated against Table 1. Along the way: found and fixed a real EMRI-overproduction
bug (relaxation-walk substepping), and traced a large, still-open modeling ambiguity
in N26's own Eq. 22 (does the relaxation timescale's <M_avg>/rho use star-only mass,
per Rose et al. 2022's explicit "single-mass system" derivation, or a BH-inclusive
weighted average, per N26's own broader phrasing and its new two-component density
apparatus?). Both readings are textually defensible and give qualitatively different
behavior (star-only keeps the merger channel alive but produces a heavy-tailed
runaway-growth residual; BH-inclusive suppresses runaway growth but reproduces the
original EMRI-overproduction defect almost exactly). Adopted star-only as the
default, flagged as open, and made it a first-class config toggle
(`relaxation_mass_weighting`) rather than picking silently -- every subsequent scan
reports both readings. Also resolved the Coulomb logarithm (ln(M_bullet/M_star),
from Bar-Or/Kupi/Alexander 2013 and Vasiliev 2017) and flagged a genuine
1/3-vs-1/2 exponent discrepancy between N26's printed Eq. 21 and its own cited
source (Volonteri et al. 2013 / Bardeen 1970).

PHASE 4 -- critical-mass threshold scan (N26's first open question)

Does a sharp mass-distribution threshold separate "this environment reliably forms
IMBHs" from "it never does"? Built a log-uniform mass family generalizing H18's own
functional form, scanned across both Eq. 22 readings. Three passes:
- Pass 1 (9 grid points x 3 seeds x both readings): found the threshold's *existence*
  is not robust to the Eq. 22 ambiguity -- star_only shows a smooth 0%-12.7% rise;
  bh_inclusive stays flat at ~0% across the whole range.
- Pass 2 (8 seeds instead of 3, per paper/methodology.md's Gate 3 sample-size rule):
  overturned, not just tightened, the pass-1 location claim. What looked like a
  saturated threshold at 3 seeds turned out to be a gradual, unsaturated crossover
  (~20-32 Msun) at 8 -- the exact failure mode Gate 3 exists to catch.
- Pass 3: tested whether N26's own primordial-binary-merger fraction (0% vs 15%)
  shifts that crossover. It doesn't, measurably (Fisher's exact p=1.0) -- a real,
  well-evidenced null result, not just an unexamined absence of signal.

`paper/methodology.md` (new): a 9-gate checklist, built from practices this project
was already using ad hoc, for when a secondary finding graduates from "preliminary
observation" to "reportable result" -- primary-source grounding, noise-floor
comparison, adequate sample size for the specific claim, mechanism not just pattern,
an adversarial falsification pass, an internal consistency cross-check, an explicit
scope statement, framing discipline (never imply N26 is "wrong" without checking
their own cited sources), and a reproducibility artifact. Every finding from Phase 4
onward is scored against this checklist before being written up as a result.

PHASE 5 -- SMBH-mass generalization scan (N26's second open question) -- this commit

Does the paper's result hold for SMBHs other than the Milky Way's 4e6 Msun? Scanned
7 SMBH masses log-spaced across 3 decades (1.26e5-1.26e8 Msun, centered on and
including N26's own fiducial value exactly), holding the cluster's structural
profile fixed (N26 gives no prescription for how it would scale -- flagged
explicitly as our own extension, not from N26), H18 IC, both Eq. 22 readings, 8
seeds/point from the start = 112 runs. Two numerical issues caught and fixed by
smoke-testing before spending real compute: an a_min/density scaling bug (fixed via
a floor-only clamp, `population.a_min_safety_bound`), and a genuine, severe
low-mass/star_only convergence problem, decided (with the user) to track as data
(`hit_step_ceiling`) rather than route around.

Two findings, both scored against all 9 methodology.md gates:
1. The qualitative Eq. 22 dependence generalizes across the full 3-decade range:
   under star_only, literally every one of 56 runs produced an IMBH; under
   bh_inclusive, the population fraction exceeding 100 Msun stayed under 0.5% at
   every single grid point. This is a direct, conditional answer to N26's own
   question -- not a Milky Way coincidence, contingent on the same open Eq. 22
   ambiguity as everything else in this project. The 4e6 anchor point reproduces
   Phase 3's own H18 validation bit-for-bit.
2. A new, non-monotonic runaway-growth "sweet spot" 3-10x below the Milky Way mass:
   counter to the naive "smaller SMBH = worse" guess, catastrophic growth (BHs
   reaching millions of Msun, in several runs exceeding the SMBH itself) is absent
   at the lowest mass tested, peaks in a specific band, and vanishes again at and
   above the anchor -- traced to two competing sigma-dependent effects (relaxation-
   driven EMRI removal vs. collision/GW-capture growth efficiency) that happen to
   cancel at both ends of the tested range but not in the middle. Explicitly and
   heavily scoped as an artifact of holding cluster density fixed while shrinking
   the SMBH, not a claim about real lower-mass galactic nuclei.
